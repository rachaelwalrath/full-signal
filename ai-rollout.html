Outcomes we design for

What the firm has at the end

  1. 01

    An inventory of what is in use, and a decision. Every tool your people already use, on which plan, with which data. Then one choice for the firm, on a plan that does not train on your clients' information.

  2. 02

    A written AI policy that matches your regulator. Approved tools, approved uses, what is off limits, who verifies what, how clients are told. One page your partners will sign and your newest hire can follow.

  3. 03

    A setup that knows your firm. Your templates, your standard letters, your intake questions and your house style loaded once, so the answers sound like you and not like the internet.

  4. 04

    Everyone trained, hands-on, on your own documents. Not a webinar. Two sessions where each person leaves with their own repeat task running.

  5. 05

    A named champion and a verification rule. One person inside the firm who owns the setup after we leave, and a rule for what gets a second read before it goes out.

  6. 06

    A number. Three repeat tasks timed before we start and again at thirty days, so you and your partners know what changed in hours.

How it works

Four to eight weeks, in this order

  1. 01

    The call. Thirty minutes. We find out what the firm already uses, who is worried about what, and which three tasks to time.

  2. 02

    Inventory and policy. Week one. We map what is in use, draft the policy against your licensing body's guidance, and you choose the plan.

  3. 03

    Setup and training. Weeks two to four. The firm's setup is built, then every person is trained on it with their own work.

  4. 04

    Thirty days on. The three tasks are timed again, the champion takes over, and you decide whether a monthly tune-up is worth it.

Questions people ask

Do we have to switch to Claude?

No. The rollout works with the tools the firm already pays for where they fit, and the policy and training cover whatever you use. We recommend a plan that does not train on your data; which one is a conversation, not a condition.

Our regulator has published AI guidance. Does the policy follow it?

Yes. The policy is drafted against your licensing body's guidance and your state's, and it is written so a partner can sign it and a new hire can follow it.

We are smaller than ten people.

Say so on the call. A firm of eight with a real team is often a fit; a solo practice is better served by the AI Assessment.

What about staff who will not use it?

Everyone is trained on their own repeat task, not on a demo. The person who has their Tuesday report running in eleven minutes stops arguing. The policy covers the rest.

Do you build it or do we?

Both. We build the firm's setup and the policy with you in the room, train every person on it, and hand it to a named champion. You own it afterwards.
