Why Claude Gives You Generic Answers, and the Setup That Fixes It

Claude trainingOct 1, 20264 min read
Rachael WalrathFounder & Principal Consultant, Full Signal

Most people use Claude like a search box. They type a question, get a polished but generic answer, and quietly decide AI is overhyped.

The problem usually isn't the model. It's that Claude starts every conversation knowing nothing about you, your clients, or how you like to work.

The fix isn't writing cleverer prompts. It's setup.

The 5-part Signal Setup framework

This is the framework we use to run our own business with Claude. Each part builds on the one before it.

1. Profile

Your profile tells Claude who you are, what you do, and how you want answers. It's the single change that makes every conversation start smarter. Write it once, test it, and improve it over time.

2. Projects

A Project is one home for each client, team, or workstream. Your files, notes, and instructions live there, so you stop re-explaining context every time you open a new chat.

3. Skills

Skills turn the tasks you repeat every week into reusable instructions. Think follow-up emails from call notes, proposal first drafts, or a weekly client update. You set up the task once and run it in seconds after that.

4. Connections

Connections link Claude to your email, calendar, and files. This is where Claude stops being a chat window and starts working with your real information.

5. Rhythm

Claude keeps changing, and so does your business. Rhythm means a short monthly review: what's working, what to retire, and one new workflow to add.

Where to start today

You don't need all five parts to see a difference. Three changes you can make today:

  1. Write a real profile. Include your role, your clients, and how you like answers written.
  2. Create one Project for your biggest client or workstream, and add the files Claude needs.
  3. Turn one weekly task into a Skill. Pick something you do at least once a week.
